Inside a fully charged lead acid cell, the plates are suspended in a bath of sulfuric acid and distilled water. This solution has a specific gravity of roughly one point two eight zero. Suppose your solar array gets snowed under and your state of charge drops to fifty percent. The specific gravity plummets to one point one five zero, driving the freezing point up to minus fifteen degrees Celsius. Drop the state of charge down to twenty percent, and that freezing point climbs to a staggering minus eight degrees Celsius.</p><p>When ambient air drops to minus thirty, that diluted, water-rich electrolyte undergoes phase change and freezes solid. Physics dictates that water expands by roughly nine percent as it turns to ice. This generates thousands of pounds of hydrostatic pressure per square inch inside the battery. This represents a loss of over seventy-five percent of your stored energy overnight.</p><p>Things get worse if your solar panels are buried in a snowstorm.
